## 1. Biometrics: error statistics and context (Ch. 17)
- **FAR/FRR, ROC and EER.** `[from source]` The trade-off between false acceptance and false rejection is the *receiver operating characteristic* (a radar term); banks call them "fraud" and "insult"; EER is the point at which they are equal; signature tablet: EER ≤ 1% at best; the UK banks' target in the 90s (1% fraud, 0.01% insult) was beyond the state of the art; the 1% EER was obtained *by excluding "goats"*, hiding social exclusion (SE3 p.611-612). Biometrics is more robust in assisted operation than in unattended operation (SE3 p.612, 634).
- **Birthday theorem in biometrics (identification vs verification).** `[from source]` With 10,000 biometrics there are ~50,000,000 pairs; even with a FAR of 1 in 1 million, the probability of at least one false match exceeds 1/2 with a little over a thousand enrollees; book formula: N > √(1.386 / f); with f = 10⁻⁶ it gives 1177 people (692,076 pairs; 0.999999^692,076 = 0.500); hence 1:N identification is much harder than 1:1 verification (SE3 p.632 and note 4). Use `se3_calc.py biometric-birthday`.
- **Combining biometrics** usually improves FAR or FRR and worsens the other; "lambs" and "wolves"; test with large and diverse populations (SE3 p.632).
- **Templates are not irreversible hashes:** Adler's *hill-climbing* attack reconstructs the target face (SE3 p.632-633).
- **Fingerprints:** one finger to verify; ten to check against millions (DHS: with 6,000,000 wanted persons, false positives 0.31% and miss 4% in 2004 → 10-prints); EER < 1% per finger; 20% failed 10-print enrollment in the UK (SE3 p.619). Attacks: gelatin (Matsumoto 2002, 11 readers fooled), tape, masterprint (6% of users), fingerprints from photos (SE3 p.620-621). Deterrent effect on welfare (SE3 p.621).
- **Iris:** 256-byte code; zero FAR in the laboratory; EER better than 1 in 1 million; in practice FRR 4-9% and 10% enrollment failure; Aadhaar with >1 billion (SE3 p.625-627).
- **Face:** humans err on photo-ID (Westminster experiment); they were "security theatre" (Newham, Tampa); CNNs since 2012 cut error by an order of magnitude (NIST FRVT 2018: galleries of 12 million, miss ~0.1%; ~5% of images require human adjudication); measurable racial bias; human + algorithm is best (SE3 p.612-615).
- **Handwritten signature:** professional examiners err 6.5% and laypeople 38.3%; the legal context and liability weigh in (SE3 p.609-611).
- **What goes wrong** (SE3 p.630-633): bugs and complacency ("any system held to be infallible makes operators careless"); environment (noise, alcohol, stress); changes of assumptions (closed→open, assisted→unattended, cooperative→recalcitrant, verification→identification); freshness and recordings; defenses: unpredictable challenge in voice, video with colors changing on the screen; "goats" is a discriminatory term and socially regressive systems deserve resistance; collusion; coercion; when human and machine disagree, design to support human cognition.
- **Summary:** three systems at scale (fingerprint on phones, iris in India/Middle East, face with CNNs); context matters; forensic use is problematic; debate over mass facial recognition (SE3 p.633-634).

## 2. Tamper resistance (Ch. 18)
- `[from source]` Devices: EMV/SIM, TPM, HSMs, enclaves, accessory-control chips; many are insecure (class breaks); the best resist all known physical attacks and fall only to insecure software or interfaces (SE3 p.636-637). If the computer does not resist physical tampering, it is enough to change the software; ATM = PC in a safe (SE3 p.637). Historical goal: "reduce the street value of key material to zero" (SE3 p.638).
- **The trusted interface problem.** `[from source]` None of these devices has a truly trustworthy user interface; a US$2000 HSM or US$2 smartcard depends on the PC that shows "pay US$67.99" and sends "refinance my house"; qualified signature = "I agree to be unlimitedly liable"; researchers created a PDF "signed by Amazon" with a US$1 trillion refund and bypassed validation in 21 of 22 viewers; hardware wallets suffer *evil maid* (SE3 p.667-668).
- **Market for lemons and evaluation games.** `[from source]` Few products at a high level; "easy" laboratories; the certificate covers only part (FIPS 4758 covered the hardware; CC level 6 covered only the OS); banks use secure processors for risk "dumping" (SE3 p.669). Obscurity, NDAs and air-gapped environments as a political dispute; almost no attack used inside information (SE3 p.669-670). SIM swap: India shares liability and checks the fingerprint in Aadhaar (SE3 p.670-671).
- **What is worth protecting (SE3 p.671-672):** (1) tie processing to a physical token, (2) keep value counters, (3) reduce the need to trust human operators, (4) root of trust for secure boot, (5) control overproduction by contractors ("third shift"), (6) counterfeit parts and hardware trojans. Security properties provided independently of the reliability of the environment; "security is not fairy dust"; industry can lobby for mandates (SE3 p.672). Summary: a useful component, rarely a complete solution (SE3 p.673).
