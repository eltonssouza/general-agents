# 11. Banks, accounting, internal controls and payments (Ch. 12)

Convention: "SE3 p.N" = PDF page; printed = PDF − 41. Reading: p.446-501 in full; p.502-510 (SIM swap, liability, APP fraud, M-Pesa, summary) NOT read. Everything about EMV, contactless limits, fraud and cases is from 2020 (`[DATED]`).

## 1. Why study banks
`[from source]` Professional competence (fraud, internal controls, SOX/GLBA/PCI); accounting drove computing; a model in which confidentiality plays a small role and the integrity and immutability of records is central; the bank must prevent fraud between customers and by staff, with evidence good enough that nobody accuses falsely (SE3 p.446-447). Pioneer of *dual control / multi-party authorisation (MPA)* (SE3 p.447).

## 2. Accounting and Clark-Wilson (SE3 p.447-452)
- Double-entry bookkeeping: each transaction in two ledgers; the ledgers must sum to zero; ledgers kept by different clerks; most frauds require collusion; random audits (SE3 p.448-449). Separation of duties can be just a UI "skin" with data open to technicians (SE3 p.450).
- Core banking structures: account master file, ledgers, journals, audit trail; balancing as an alarm; programmers do not create money; production without a development environment ("under strain in DevOps") (SE3 p.450).
- **Clark-Wilson (1987):** UDI → CDI; IVPs; TPs; append-only audit trail; access by triples (subject, TP, CDI). The nine rules: (1) an IVP validates the integrity of any CDI; (2) a TP preserves integrity; (3) a CDI only changes via a TP; (4) subjects initiate only certain TPs on certain CDIs; (5) triples enforce separation of duties; (6) special TPs convert UDIs into CDIs; (7) each TP writes enough to reconstruct it to an append-only CDI; (8) the system authenticates subjects; (9) only special subjects (security officers) alter authorization lists (SE3 p.451). It maintains state; it does not guarantee correctness (paying into the wrong account); it does not say how to design the separation of duties (SE3 p.451-452).

## 3. Designing internal controls (SE3 p.452-456, 462-464)
- The Big Four push ~300 controls; many are formal compliance; SOX (executives certify; whistleblower protection), GLBA, PCI, COSO, ISA 315, CoBIT, ISO 27001 ("almost every large breach involves a firm with ISO 27001") (SE3 p.452-453, 455, 463).
- **Dual control vs functional separation** and the **prevent – detect – recover** model: when detection is slow and recovery hard (letter of guarantee), strengthen prevention; if prevention is hard, make detection fast and recovery vigorous (count the till every day) (SE3 p.453-454). Controls must match the culture; job rotation and forced vacations of 1-2 weeks (SE3 p.454).
- **Dual control in engineering** (code in production): interfaces are single points; split administration is tedious; requires persistent state; there are always trusted sysadmins; DevOps changes where the trust lies; emergencies require logging and auditing; the best engineers know more than the auditors (SE3 p.455-456). **Functional prevent-detect-recover controls are often more important than shared control** because they separate know-how and access (SE3 p.456). "In a real bank there are 30-40 people you have to trust: know who they are, minimize the number, pay them well and watch them discreetly" (SE3 p.456).
- **Finding the weak points:** "a trusted party is one who can hurt you"; ask employees "if you wanted to defraud the company, how would you do it?"; think about people, incentives and norms; no policy gets full compliance; "it is a terrible idea to declare a system fail-safe (a prior probability of zero does not change with evidence)"; contribute to profit (SE3 p.463-464).

## 4. Insider and executive fraud (SE3 p.456-463)
- ~1% of staff dismissed per year; cases: Lloyds/"Fizzy" (£113m; charged the victim's authenticator code), HSBC/AT&T (US$20m), spear phishing of two employee accounts at mid-size companies (SE3 p.456-457). Executives: Barings, Equity Funding, Maxwell, Wells Fargo US$3bn (2020) (SE3 p.457-458). Of 230 corporate frauds (1996-2004), auditors detected 14% and the SEC 6%; employees 19% (SE3 p.458). Audit and consulting were separated in the UK in 2020 (SE3 p.458-459).
- **Post Office / Horizon:** 587 sub-postmasters won £58m in 2019; "not remotely robust"; accounting systems are presumed correct by law (SE3 p.459-460). Others: NatWest 2012; project costs (SE3 p.460).
- **Ecological validity:** McKesson & Robbins 1938, salad oil 1963, Enron, Wirecard (€2.1bn that could not be found, a quarter of declared assets; screenshots instead of confirmation with the bank) — "checking balances abroad is audit 101"; blockchain does not tell you whether the assets exist (SE3 p.461-462).

## 5. Interbank payments and SWIFT (SE3 p.465-471)
- Telegraph and *test keys*; SWIFT: bilateral MAC with keys in two components sent in opposite directions, authenticity independent of SWIFT; **non-repudiation through logs of independent RGPs**; three people (enter, check, authorize), but the checker can alter, so it is dual control; daily reconciliation (SE3 p.466-468). SWIFT I: 20 years without external fraud; 512-bit RSA keys factored by students (SE3 p.468).
- Real fraud uses business processes: Bangladesh Bank 2016 (US$81m diverted from the NY Fed; 30 transactions of US$851m blocked by manual review) — "it was not an attack on SWIFT, but on the bank's gateway" (SE3 p.469); Rifkin 1979 (authorization code overheard by phone), Johannesburg 1986, letters of guarantee: **"be alert to anything that defeats dual control; the core is the design of the business process"** (SE3 p.470-471).

## 6. ATMs and PINs (SE3 p.471-479)
- IBM 3624 method: the PIN key encrypts the PAN, decimalizes and truncates (natural PIN); offset without a cryptographic function (SE3 p.471-472). HSM/SCD; dual control in a ceremony with two components by different people (managers often hand both to the engineer); switches translate PINs (SE3 p.472-473). Real doomsday: Postbank (South Africa), PIN key copied from a laptop: R56m in 56,000 transactions; 12m cards reissued (SE3 p.473-474).
- **What went wrong:** processing errors (retransmission duplicated debits), mail theft (~30% of UK losses), insiders, Stone (card with swapped account), test dispense with 14 digits in a manual, default master password, Lebanese loop, false terminals, jackpotting (SE3 p.474-477). **Designers' mistakes:** worrying about clever criminals instead of the fact that internal "customers" could not use the mechanisms; not noticing which attacks would be industrialized (SE3 p.477-478).
- **Incentives:** Regulation E (US: the bank must prove customer fraud) vs "infallible system" in the UK (Munden, Badger) — "if a system is to provide evidence, dual control is not enough: the required property is **non-repudiation** and resisting examination by hostile experts" (SE3 p.478-479).

## 7. Cards, EMV, online (SE3 p.479-501)
- CVV, hot lists, floor limits, chargebacks, CNP and liability shift (SE3 p.480); PCI DSS (cardholder data encrypted; CVV/PIN must not be stored; secure SDLC since 2017) (SE3 p.482-483); progressive guessing (SE3 p.483-484); 3DS (SE3 p.484).
- **Fraud engines:** anomaly vs abuse detection; false positives; "the signals matter more than the classifier"; the CMO vs CFO rule (4%→3% of declined baskets = 1% more sales) (SE3 p.485-486).
- **EMV:** ARQC/ARPC, SDA/DDA/CDA, iCVV (channel separation) (SE3 p.486-492); CVM list unauthenticated in EMV v1 (SE3 p.489-490); **No-PIN attack** (unverified flag; only stopped working in the UK in 2017) (SE3 p.492-493); **pre-play attack** ("unpredictable number" was a 16-bit counter; ~half of the Cambridge ATMs used counters; the EMV test only required three different numbers) (SE3 p.493-494); relay (SE3 p.490). Contactless: Tesco Bank 2016 (£2.2m from 8,261 accounts; £16.4m fine; fraud engine and weekend procedures could not cope) (SE3 p.496-497); SPoC (SE3 p.497-498).
- Online: phishing, mules, mule banks suffer less fraud, **prevent-detect-recover**; CAP (a calculator that checks the PIN may have led to homicides: "negligent design"); banking malware and man-in-the-browser → authenticate the last 4 digits of new payees; SMS 2FA → SIM swap (SE3 p.498-501).
