## Learning × pure optimization
- `[from source]` (DLB p.290-292) J(θ) is optimized **indirectly**, hoping to improve the test measure P. Empirical risk (eq. 8.3) is prone to overfitting; losses such as 0-1 have no useful derivative: a **surrogate loss** is used (NLL in place of 0-1); the test 0-1 error can keep falling after the training error reaches zero (it pushes the classes apart). Training normally **does not stop at a local minimum**: it stops by *early stopping*, still with large gradients.
- `[from source]` (DLB p.292-297) *Batch* (deterministic), *minibatch* and *online*. Standard error σ/√n: 100 vs 10,000 examples costs 100× more and reduces the standard error only 10× (p.293). Minibatch size factors (p.294): larger batches give a more exact gradient with less than linear returns; multicore is underused with small batches; memory grows with the batch; powers of 2 may be faster on GPU (typically from 32 to 256); small batches can regularize (better generalization sometimes with batch size 1, but it requires a small learning rate and a lot of time). Gradient-only methods tolerate batches of ~100; second-order ones ask for ~10,000 (p.294-295). Minibatches must be random and independent; shuffling once is usually enough; never shuffling seriously hurts (p.295). Only the first epoch follows the unbiased gradient of the generalization error (p.296-297); with enormous datasets, the problem becomes underfitting and efficiency (p.297).

## Challenges (8.2)
- `[from source]` (DLB p.297-298) **Ill-conditioning**: the term ½ε²gᵀHg can exceed εgᵀg (eq. 8.10); monitor gᵀg and gᵀHg; the gradient may **not shrink** and gᵀHg may grow by more than an order of magnitude during a successful training run (Fig. 8.1).
- `[from source]` (DLB p.298-300) **Local minima**: non-identifiability (weight-space symmetry: n!ᵐ permutations; scaling by α and 1/α in ReLU/maxout); it is now suspected that in large networks most local minima have low cost (p.300). **Diagnostic test:** plot the gradient norm over time; if it does not shrink, the problem is neither a local minimum nor another critical point (p.300).
- `[from source]` (DLB p.300-303) In high dimension **saddles** are far more common than local minima; unmodified Newton can jump to a saddle, which explains why second-order methods did not replace GD (p.303); *saddle-free Newton* (Dauphin 2014); Fig. 8.2: SGD escapes a high-cost saddle near the initialization; flat regions have zero gradient and Hessian.
- `[from source]` (DLB p.303-304) **Cliffs** and exploding gradients come from multiplying several large weights, more common in RNNs; the *gradient clipping* heuristic (section 10.11.1) reduces the step.
- `[from source]` (DLB p.304-305) Long-term dependencies: W^t = V diag(λ)^t V⁻¹ (eq. 8.11): eigenvalues with modulus >1 explode, <1 vanish (*vanishing/exploding gradient*); it is like the power method; deep feedforward networks avoid the problem because they do not reuse W (Sussillo 2014).

## Basic algorithms
- `[from source]` (DLB p.309-311) **SGD** (Alg. 8.1) with learning rate εₖ; sufficient convergence condition Σεₖ = ∞ and Σεₖ² < ∞ (8.12-8.13); in practice it decays linearly until iteration τ: εₖ = (1−α)ε₀ + αε_τ, α = k/τ (eq. 8.14); τ ≈ iterations for a few hundred passes through the training set, ε_τ ≈ 1% of ε₀; ε₀ by trial and error watching learning curves ("more of an art than a science"); ε too large causes violent oscillations, too small stalls; the best ε₀ is usually larger than the best one after ~100 iterations (p.310). Excess error O(1/√k) (convex) or O(1/k) (strongly convex); Cramér-Rao bounds the generalization error at O(1/k) (p.310-311).
- `[from source]` (DLB p.311-314) **Momentum** (Polyak 1964): v ← αv − ε∇(...); θ ← θ + v (eq. 8.15-8.16, Alg. 8.2); terminal step ε‖g‖/(1−α) (eq. 8.17): α = 0.9 multiplies the maximum velocity by 10; common values α = 0.5, 0.9, 0.99 (p.313); physical analogy with viscous drag (p.313-314).

## Initialization
- `[from source]` (DLB p.316-318) The initial point can decide whether training converges, how fast, and even generalization; all that is known for certain is that it is necessary to **break the symmetry** between units (different weights). Random Gaussian or uniform weights (the choice matters little; the **scale** matters a lot); heuristic constant biases; large weights give a stronger signal but risk explosion and saturation; small ones regularize. Heuristics: U(−1/√m, 1/√m) for m inputs and the **normalized initialization of Glorot and Bengio** U(−√(6/(m+n)), √(6/(m+n))) (eq. 8.23, p.318), derived assuming a chain of matrix multiplications **without nonlinearities**; Saxe 2013 recommends random orthogonal matrices with gain g.
- `[from source]` (DLB p.321) LSTM *forget gate* bias = 1 (Jozefowicz 2015); precision/variance parameters initialized at 1; initialize with an unsupervised model or a supervised model on a related task.
- `[DATED]` The book gives no activation-specific initialization rules (later variations): treat as a gap.

## Adaptive rates
- `[from source]` (DLB p.321-323) The learning rate is among the hardest hyperparameters; *delta-bar-delta* (full batch only). **AdaGrad** (Duchi 2011): r ← r + g⊙g; Δθ = −ε/(δ + √r)⊙g, δ ≈ 10⁻⁷ (Alg. 8.4): accumulating from the beginning can reduce the effective rate too much in deep networks.
- `[from source]` (DLB p.322-325) **RMSProp** (Hinton 2012): r ← ρr + (1−ρ)g⊙g; Δθ = −ε/√(δ + r)⊙g, δ ≈ 10⁻⁶ (Alg. 8.5; with Nesterov, Alg. 8.6); "one of the go-to methods".
- `[from source]` (DLB p.323-326) **Adam** (Kingma and Ba 2014; Alg. 8.7): s ← ρ₁s + (1−ρ₁)g; r ← ρ₂r + (1−ρ₂)g⊙g; ŝ = s/(1−ρ₁ᵗ); r̂ = r/(1−ρ₂ᵗ); Δθ = −ε ŝ/(√r̂ + δ); suggested defaults ε = 0.001, ρ₁ = 0.9, ρ₂ = 0.999, δ = 10⁻⁸ `[DATED]`; bias correction in the moments; "fairly robust" to the hyperparameters, although ε sometimes needs to change (p.324).
- `[from source]` (DLB p.324-325) There is no consensus on the best algorithm; the most used: SGD, SGD+momentum, RMSProp (± momentum), AdaDelta and Adam; the choice depends on the user's familiarity.

## Approximate second order and meta-algorithms
- `[from source]` (DLB p.325-329) Newton (eq. 8.26-8.27, Alg. 8.8) requires positive definite H; regularize with αI (Levenberg-Marquardt, eq. 8.28); O(k³) inversion with k parameters makes it infeasible (p.328); conjugate gradients (eq. 8.29-8.30); L-BFGS reduces BFGS memory (p.332).
- `[from source]` (DLB p.332-336) **Batch normalization** (Ioffe and Szegedy 2015): adaptive reparametrization (not an optimization algorithm) to update all layers at the same time without higher-order effects; H′ = (H − μ)/σ (eq. 8.35), μ = (1/m)ΣHᵢ,: and σ = √(δ + (1/m)Σ(H−μ)²ᵢ) (8.36-8.37, δ ≈ 10⁻⁸); backpropagation goes **through** the computation of μ and σ; at test time, moving averages from training are used; γH′ + β preserves expressive power; applied to XW + b, with the bias omitted (redundant with β); in CNNs the same μ and σ are used at all spatial positions of a *feature map*.
- `[from source]` (DLB p.336-337) *Coordinate descent* (bad with strongly coupled variables) and **Polyak averaging** (average of the iterates; in non-convex settings, exponential moving average θ̂ = αθ̂ + (1−α)θ, eq. 8.39).
