# Web Accessibility Fundamentals (W3C WAI)

Source: **WAI**, *Introduction to Web Accessibility* (8 pages; pages 7-8 are almost only footer and links). `(WAI p.N)` citations = PDF page. Text rewritten in the collection's own words.

## 1. Thesis and context

- **Thesis** [from source]: when sites and tools are well designed and coded, people with disabilities can use them; today many have barriers that make use difficult or impossible for some. Accessibility benefits individuals, businesses and society, and international standards define what is needed (WAI p.1).
- **The web was designed for everyone**, regardless of hardware, software, language, location or ability. When it reaches that goal, it is accessible to different hearing, motor, visual and cognitive abilities (WAI p.2).
- **The web changes the impact of disability**, since it removes communication and interaction barriers of the physical world; but poor design recreates barriers and excludes (WAI p.2). Operational reading [inference]: the barrier arises from a design/code decision, so it can be fixed by those who design and develop.
- The page quotes a sentence by Tim Berners-Lee on the universality of the web and access by everyone as an essential aspect (WAI p.2).
- Accessibility is essential for those who want to create high-quality sites and tools without excluding users (WAI p.2).

## 2. Definition

Web accessibility = designing and developing so that people with disabilities can **perceive, understand, navigate and interact** with the web and **contribute** to it (WAI p.3). It covers **auditory, cognitive, neurological, physical, speech and visual** disabilities (WAI p.3).

## 3. Who else benefits most (WAI p.3)

1. Users of mobile phones, smartwatches, TVs and other devices with small screens or different input modes.
2. Older people, whose abilities change with age.
3. People with "temporary disabilities" (broken arm, lost glasses).
4. People with "situational limitations" (bright sunlight, an environment where audio cannot be heard).
5. Those with a slow connection or limited/expensive bandwidth.

Decision rule [inference]: when justifying accessibility to stakeholders, use this list to show that the affected audience goes beyond permanent disabilities.

## 4. Why it matters (WAI p.4-5)

- The web is a central resource in education, employment, government, commerce, health and leisure; accessibility gives equal access and opportunity (WAI p.4).
- Access to ICTs, including the web, is defined as a basic human right in the UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (UN CRPD) (WAI p.4).
- The web offers unprecedented access; barriers of print, audio and visual media are easier to overcome with web technologies (WAI p.4).
- Social inclusion also applies to older people, people in rural areas and in developing countries (WAI p.4).
- **Business case**: improves overall experience and satisfaction (especially in varied situations, diverse devices and older users), can strengthen the brand, drive innovation and extend market reach (WAI p.4).
- **Law**: accessibility is required by law in many situations (WAI p.4). The source does **not** say which laws or from which countries; there is only a pointer to an archived resource on legal and policy factors (WAI p.5). Do not fill in from memory: refer to legal counsel/the official source.

## 5. How the web becomes accessible (WAI p.5)

- It depends on **several components working together**: web technologies, browsers and other *user agents*, authoring tools and the sites (WAI p.5).
- The W3C WAI produces specifications, guidelines, techniques and resources, considered international standards; example given: WCAG 2.2, which is also the ISO/IEC 40500 standard (WAI p.5). `[DATED]` as to the version number: the page is from 2026 and cites 2.2 (WAI p.7).
- WCAG, ATAG and ARIA appear **only as names** of resources (WAI p.5). Their content is **not** in the sources.
- Some aspects are easy to understand and implement; others require more knowledge (WAI p.5).
- **Decision rule** [from source]: building accessibility in **from the start** is the most efficient and effective, avoiding rework (WAI p.5).

## 6. How to evaluate (WAI p.6)

- Evaluate **early and throughout** development to find problems when they are easier to handle (WAI p.6).
- Simple steps, such as changing browser settings, help evaluate some aspects; full conformance evaluation requires more effort (WAI p.6). The page does **not** say which settings.
- Tools help, but **none alone determines** whether a site meets the guidelines; **qualified human evaluation** is needed (WAI p.6).
- Consequence for this agent [inference]: scripts and checklists are triage; never declare "conformant" or "accessible" based on a tool alone.
