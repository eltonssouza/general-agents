## 1. Ch.14: unsupervised learning and metrics
- `[from source]` Unsupervised = learning from `{xi}` without labels; uses: generate, manipulate, interpolate, compress, cluster, detect outliers (UDL p.282). Many families map between data `x` and latents `z` of lower dimension (UDL p.282-283); k-means maps x→z, and in generators z→x.
- `[from source]` Taxonomy (fig. 14.1, UDL p.283): GANs generate but do not assign probability; flows, VAEs and diffusion are probabilistic and train with `L[φ] = −Σ log Pr(xi|φ)` (eq. 14.1). The transformer decoder is a probabilistic generative model without latent variables (footnote, UDL p.283).
- `[from source]` Desirable properties: efficient sampling, high quality, coverage, well-behaved and disentangled latent space, efficient likelihood (UDL p.284); fig. 14.3 (UDL p.285) evaluates the four families and the book admits it is "debatable" and that none has all of them.
- `[from source]` Metrics (UDL p.285-287): test likelihood (only in flows is it exact and efficient); **Inception Score** `exp[(1/I) Σ DKL[Pr(y|x*) || Pr(y)]]` (eq. 14.2-14.3), only for ImageNet and sensitive to the classifier; **FID** (Gaussians fitted to the Inception activations; uses the Fréchet distance); **manifold precision/recall** (hyperspheres up to the k-th neighbor) (UDL p.285-288).

## 2. GANs (ch.15)
- `[from source]` Generator `x* = g[z, θ]`, discriminator `f[x, φ]` that classifies real × generated; GANs do not define a distribution and do not evaluate probability (UDL p.289-290). 1D example `x* = z + θ` with initial θ of 3.0 (UDL p.290, eq. 15.1).
- `[from source]` Discriminator loss (BCE, eq. 15.3), minimax `θ̂ = argmax_θ min_φ {...}` (eq. 15.4), Nash equilibrium; at convergence `sig[f] = 0.5` (UDL p.291). The two training losses: `L[φ] = Σ −log[1−sig f(g(z_j))] − Σ log sig f(x_i)` and `L[θ] = Σ log[1−sig f(g(z_j))]` (UDL p.291-292, eq. 15.5). The "non-saturating" variant does not appear in the pages read.
- `[from source]` DCGAN: 100-dimensional uniform z → 4×4×1,024 → four fractionally strided convolutions with stride 0.5 → 64×64×3 with tanh; to train reliably it required strided convolutions, BatchNorm (except last/first layer), leaky ReLU in the discriminator and Adam with a lower-than-usual momentum (UDL p.292-293). Failures: **mode dropping** and **mode collapse** (UDL p.294).
- `[from source]` Analysis: with the optimal discriminator `Pr(real|x̃) = Pr(x)/(Pr(x*)+Pr(x))` (eq. 15.7) the loss is proportional to the Jensen-Shannon divergence (eq. 15.9), whose first term measures quality (samples where there is no data) and the second coverage — the latter does not depend on the generator, which explains mode dropping (UDL p.295-296). **Vanishing gradients** when the distributions are disjoint (low-dimensional subspaces) (UDL p.296-297, fig. 15.7).
- `[from source]` **Wasserstein**: transport distance; discrete case `min_P Σ P_ij |i−j|` with marginal constraints (eq. 15.10-15.11), dual with `|f_{i+1} − f_i| < 1` (eq. 15.12-15.13); continuous with Lipschitz constraint (eq. 15.14-15.15); **WGAN**: `L[φ] = Σ f[g[z_j,θ],φ] − Σ f[x_i,φ]` with `||∂f/∂x|| < 1`, by weight clipping to [−0.01; 0.01] or gradient penalty (WGAN-GP) (UDL p.297-299, eq. 15.16-15.17). `[checked in dl_calc.py divergence]` for discrete cases.
- `[from source]` Quality: progressive growing (4×4 → 8×8…), minibatch discrimination, truncation (reject z far from the mean: quality × diversity) (UDL p.300-302). Conditional: cGAN `g[z,c,θ]`, `f[x,c,φ]`; ACGAN (C+1 outputs); InfoGAN (discovered attributes) (UDL p.302-304). Image translation: pix2pix (U-Net, ℓ1 content loss and PatchGAN), adversarial loss in SRGAN (content, VGG and adversarial), CycleGAN (cycle consistency) (UDL p.304-310). StyleGAN: 4×4×512 constant, noise injected at each scale and style `w` via a 7-layer FC network with AdaIN (UDL p.309-311).
- Notes (UDL p.312-314): the Nash equilibrium is not always reached in practice; LSGAN, f-GAN, relativistic formulations; spectral normalization; tricks (top-K, label smoothing, history of generated samples); actual diversity is lower than assumed (Arora & Zhang 2017: support ≈ 400,000 < training set in DCGAN).

## 3. Normalizing flows (ch.16)
- `[from source]` `x = f[z, φ]` with simple base density; probability `Pr(x|φ) = |∂f/∂z|⁻¹ Pr(z)` (eq. 16.1; multivariate, Jacobian determinant, eq. 16.3); `z = f⁻¹[x]` is the normalizing direction (UDL p.317-321). Training: NLL `Σ log|∂f[z_i]/∂z_i| − log Pr(z_i)` (eq. 16.2, 16.8) (UDL p.320-322).
- `[from source]` Four requirements for the layers: expressivity, invertibility, efficient inverse and efficient determinant (UDL p.322). Layers: linear (inversion O[D³]; triangular O[D²]; LU parameterization `Ω = PL(U+D)`), element-wise (determinant = product), **coupling** (`h'1 = h1`, `h'2 = g[h2, φ[h1]]`), **autoregressive** (parallel forward, sequential inverse; MAF × IAF; teacher-student trick), **residual** (iRevNet: `h'1 = h1 + f1[h2]`, `h'2 = h2 + f2[h'1]`; iResNet: contraction and Hutchinson estimator) (UDL p.323-330, eq. 16.9-16.24). Note: the "transformer" of flow jargon is NOT the transformer of ch.12 (footnote, UDL p.326).
- `[from source]` Multi-scale (UDL p.330-331); applications: the only family with exact log-likelihood; anomaly detection with caution; GLOW (256×256×3, affine coupling, LU 1×1 convolutions, dequantization, temperature); samples inferior to those of GANs/diffusion; approximating another density (student/teacher, reverse KL) (UDL p.331-334). Invertible flows reduce training memory from O[K] to O[1] (UDL p.335).

## 4. VAEs (ch.17)
- `[from source]` Latent variable model: `Pr(x) = ∫Pr(x|z)Pr(z)dz` (eq. 17.1-17.2); mixture of Gaussians as an example (eq. 17.3-17.4); nonlinear model: `Pr(z) = Norm[0,I]`, `Pr(x|z,φ) = Norm[f[z,φ], σ²I]` (eq. 17.5-17.6, UDL p.340-342). The VAE is an architecture that helps learn the model, which in the end contains neither the "variational" nor the "autoencoder" parts (UDL p.340).
- `[from source]` Training: the integral is intractable (eq. 17.8-17.9); ELBO via Jensen's inequality `log E[y] ≥ E[log y]` (eq. 17.10-17.15): `ELBO[θ,φ] = ∫ q(z|θ) log[Pr(x,z|φ)/q(z|θ)] dz` (eq. 17.16) = `log Pr(x|φ) − DKL[q || Pr(z|x,φ)]` (eq. 17.17) = reconstruction − `DKL[q(z|x,θ) || Pr(z)]` (eq. 17.18) (UDL p.344-349).
- `[from source]` Variational: `q(z|x,θ) = Norm[g_μ[x,θ], g_Σ[x,θ]]` (diagonal covariance) (eq. 17.20); Monte Carlo with one sample (eq. 17.23); KL between a normal and the standard normal `½(Tr Σ + μᵀμ − Dz − log det Σ)` (eq. 17.24); **reparameterization trick** `z* = μ + Σ^{1/2} ε*` (eq. 17.25) (UDL p.349-352). `[checked in dl_calc.py kl-normal]`.
- `[from source]` Applications and limits: approximate Pr(x) by importance sampling (eq. 17.26-17.28); vanilla samples are of low quality; sample from the aggregate posterior; modern ones only with hierarchical priors; resynthesis and Slerp; disentanglement: β-VAE (β>1 on the KL term) (UDL p.353-356). Notes: deterministic autoencoder (PCA if linear), posterior collapse (mitigate by gradually raising the KL term), VQ-VAE, IWAE (UDL p.357-360).
- `[from source]` Diffusion = hierarchical VAE with a fixed encoder (UDL p.355, p.362); see `11-difusao.md`.
