## 1. Three distinct notions (ISLR p.352)
`[from source]` ISLR separates the **maximal margin classifier** (requires separable classes), the **support vector classifier** (soft margin, non-separable case) and the **SVM** (support vector classifier with a non-linear kernel) (ISLR p.352).

## 2. Maximal margin
- `[from source]` Hyperplane in p dimensions: β₀ + β₁X₁ + … + β_pX_p = 0; the sign of the left-hand side says which side a point is on (ISLR p.353). With yᵢ ∈ {−1, 1}, a separating hyperplane satisfies yᵢ(β₀ + β₁xᵢ₁ + … + β_pxᵢₚ) > 0; one classifies by the sign of f(x*) and the magnitude of f(x*) indicates the confidence (ISLR p.355-356).
- `[from source]` There are infinitely many separating hyperplanes; the **maximal margin** one is the farthest from the training observations (the margin is the smallest perpendicular distance); it can overfit with large p; the points on the margin are the **support vectors**, and only they determine the hyperplane (ISLR p.356-357). Problem: maximize M subject to Σβ²ⱼ = 1 and yᵢ f(xᵢ) ≥ M (9.9-9.11) (ISLR p.357-358). If the classes are not separable, there is no solution with M > 0 (ISLR p.358).

## 3. Support vector classifier (soft margin)
- `[from source]` The maximal margin hyperplane is sensitive to individual observations (one extra observation changes everything); the soft-margin classifier tolerates some observations on the wrong side of the margin or of the hyperplane (ISLR p.359-360). Problem (9.12)-(9.15): maximize M with yᵢ f(xᵢ) ≥ M(1 − εᵢ), εᵢ ≥ 0, Σεᵢ ≤ C; εᵢ = 0: right side of the margin; εᵢ > 0: violated the margin; εᵢ > 1: wrong side of the hyperplane (ISLR p.361-362).
- `[from source]` **C is a budget of violations**: C = 0 recovers the maximal margin; with C > 0, at most C observations fall on the wrong side of the hyperplane; larger C → wider margin, more support vectors, less variance and more bias; smaller C → narrow margin, closer fit, low bias and high variance; C is chosen by cross-validation (ISLR p.362-363). Only the points on the margin or that violate it (the support vectors) affect the classifier, which makes it robust to distant points, unlike LDA (which uses all observations); logistic has low sensitivity to points far from the boundary (ISLR p.362-364).
- `[from source]` ESL: min ½‖β‖² + C Σξᵢ subject to ξᵢ ≥ 0 and yᵢ(xᵢᵀβ + β₀) ≥ 1 − ξᵢ (12.8), with C = ∞ in the separable case; two ways of defining the slack (12.5 non-convex, 12.6 convex; the "standard" one is the convex one); β̂ = Σ α̂ᵢyᵢxᵢ (12.17), with α̂ᵢ > 0 only at the support vectors (0 < α̂ᵢ < C on the margin and α̂ᵢ = C violating the margin) (ESL p.438-440). In the mixture example, C = 10,000 and C = 0.01 gave 62% and 85% of support points; **the LOO error is bounded by the proportion of support points, but the bound is loose and of little use for choosing C** (ESL p.440-441).

## 4. Kernels and SVM
- `[from source]` Enlarging the feature space (X, X², …) makes the boundary non-linear in the original space, but can generate too many features (ISLR p.364-365). The solution of the support vector classifier depends only on **inner products** ⟨xᵢ, xᵢ′⟩: f(x) = β₀ + Σ_{i∈S} αᵢ⟨x, xᵢ⟩, with αᵢ ≠ 0 only at the support vectors (ISLR p.366). Replacing the inner product by a **kernel** K(xᵢ, xᵢ′) (similarity measure) yields the SVM: f(x) = β₀ + Σ_{i∈S} αᵢK(x, xᵢ) (ISLR p.367).
- `[from source]` Linear kernel Σ x_ij x_i′j; **polynomial** (1 + Σ x_ij x_i′j)^d (d = 1 recovers the support vector classifier); **radial** exp(−γ Σ (x_ij − x_i′j)²), with local behavior (only nearby observations influence) (ISLR p.367-368). Advantage of the kernel: computational cost (only the C(n, 2) values of K) and the implicit space of the radial kernel has infinite dimension (ISLR p.368). ESL: symmetric positive semidefinite kernels; polynomial, radial and "neural network" tanh(κ₁⟨x, x′⟩ + κ₂) (12.22); for the degree-2 polynomial in 2 inputs, K = ⟨h(X), h(X′)⟩ with M = 6 basis functions (12.23) (ESL p.443).
- `[from source]` ESL clarifies: large C discourages ξᵢ > 0 and gives a wiggly boundary (overfit); small C gives a smoother boundary (ESL p.443). In the mixture, the radial kernel came close to the Bayes error (test 0.218 against Bayes 0.21) and the degree-4 polynomial gave 0.245 (ESL p.443-444). **ESL states that the initial claims are false** that the kernel property is exclusive to the SVM and that the SVM sidesteps the curse of dimensionality (ESL p.443, 450).
- `[from source]` Practice (Heart, ISLR): 297 subjects (6 with missing values removed), 207 training and 90 test; on the training set the radial kernel with γ = 10⁻¹ is almost perfect, but on the test set it is the **worst**, whereas γ = 10⁻² and 10⁻³ stay close to the support vector classifier: more flexible reduces the training error, not necessarily the test error (ISLR p.369-370).
- `[from source]` ESL "skin of the orange" (4 useful features, with and without 6 noise ones; 100 observations per class; Bayes 0.029): the linear support vector classifier errs ≈ 0.45 in both cases; the degree-2 polynomial SVM (the order of the true boundary) goes to 0.078 without noise and 0.152 with noise, whereas degree 10 rises to 0.23 and 0.43; BRUTO (adaptive additive model) barely changes with noise (0.084 and 0.090) (ESL p.450-451): the SVM is sensitive to the kernel degree and to noise features, unlike adaptive methods. The optimal C depends on the scale γ of the radial kernel (ESL p.451).

## 5. Multiclass and relation to logistic
- `[from source]` **One-versus-one** (C(K, 2) SVMs, vote) and **one-versus-all** (K SVMs; class with the largest f) (ISLR p.370-371). ESL: a classifier for each pair, the class that wins most often prevails (ESL p.457).
- `[from source]` The SVM criterion is equivalent to min Σ max[0, 1 − yᵢf(xᵢ)] + λΣβ²ⱼ (9.25): **hinge loss** + ridge penalty; small λ ≈ small C; it is zero for yᵢf(xᵢ) ≥ 1; the logistic loss is never exactly zero, but is small far from the boundary, which is why logistic and the support vector classifier give similar results; with well-separated classes the SVM tends to do better; with overlapping classes logistic is often preferred; C is **not** a "minor" tuning parameter (ISLR p.371-373). Non-linear kernels can also be used with logistic and others; *support vector regression* exists (ISLR p.373).
- `[from source]` ESL: SVM as penalization: Σ[1 − yᵢf(xᵢ)]₊ + (λ/2)‖β‖² with λ = 1/C (12.25); population minimizers: deviance → log-odds; hinge → sign[Pr(Y = +1|x) − ½] (estimates the classifier, not probabilities); squared → 2Pr − 1 (ESL p.445-446). SRM: the VC bound h ≤ R²A²; C controls an upper bound of the VC, but it is not clear that choosing C by that bound is better than CV (ESL p.457).

## Pitfalls
- Applying SVM without standardizing features `[extrapolation]` (the book shows sensitivity to the scale of the radial kernel: ESL p.451).
- Fixing C = 1 as if it were irrelevant (ISLR p.373).
- **Two conventions of "C"**: in the ISLR text, C is a *budget* of violations (larger C → wider margin, ISLR p.362); in ESL, C is a *cost* (min ½‖β‖² + CΣξ; large C discourages ξ > 0 and the margin narrows, ESL p.439, 443); and the `cost` argument of the `svm()` function in the R lab (R, `[DATED]`) behaves like ESL: small cost → wide margin, many support vectors (ISLR p.374). When switching source or software, check in which direction the parameter acts.
- Choosing γ by the training error (ISLR p.369-370).
- Expecting calibrated probabilities from the SVM: the hinge estimates the classifier (ESL p.445-446).
