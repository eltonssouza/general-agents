## 2. Audience and premise (NDDB p.10)

- A book for people who need to design pieces without design training (assistants who get handed newsletters, volunteers, small business owners, students, professionals, teachers, statisticians...).
- Premise: most people see that a page is bad but do not know how to fix it; **four basic concepts** appear in practically all well-designed work; by **naming the problem** one finds the solution.
- It declares that it does not replace design school; it promises more professional, organized, unified and interesting work, and a different eye for pages.
- Implication for the agent [inference]: the book's method is **diagnosis by names** — identify which principle is missing and act on it.

## 3. Structure according to the table of contents (pointers only)

Each principle has a chapter and a closing summary with "basic purpose", "how to get it" and "what to avoid" (NDDB p.6). Table of contents pages (NDDB p.6-9):

- Introduction p.11; the four principles p.13.
- **Proximity** p.15 (summary p.32), **Alignment** p.33 (summary p.54), **Repetition** p.55 (summary p.68), **Contrast** p.69 (summary p.84).
- Review of the four principles p.85; tests ("Little Quiz") and ad redesign p.90-91.
- **Color** p.95: color wheel, relationships (complementary, triads, analogous, hues and tints), warm vs. cool, "cmyk vs. rgb: print vs. web" p.110-112.
- Extra tips p.113: brand package, business card, letterhead and envelopes, flyers, newsletters, brochures, postcards, ads, résumés.
- **Typography**: essentials p.151; "Type (& Life)" (concord, conflict, contrast) p.167; type categories p.175; type contrasts (size, weight, structure, form, direction, color) p.187; "Does it make sense?" p.219; answers p.223.

**Note**: of these parts, **only Repetition has text in the sample**. The chapter numerals in the extracted table of contents are scrambled; it is only safe to state that "Repetition" is chapter 4 (label "4 : repetition", NDDB p.55-68).

## 5. Gaps and limits to always state

1. **Partial sample**: 29 PDF pages (of which only p.55-68 carry content) of a book whose index reaches p.239; the exact total of pages is not given in the sample.
2. **Print-centrism**: examples are printed pieces; mentions of fax, photocopies, newsprint, and fonts and tools of the period (`[DATED]` when relevant). Web only in the color "print vs web" (NDDB p.7, 236-239).
3. **No link to accessibility**: the NDDB does not mention disability, WCAG or assistive technology in the sample. The book's principle of Contrast is visual/aesthetic; it is **not** an accessibility criterion.
4. **Exercise answers** (p.223-228) absent.
5. Any data, number or rule from an absent chapter is **[extrapolation]** or must be left out.
