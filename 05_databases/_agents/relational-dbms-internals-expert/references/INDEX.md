## 5. Scripts (in `../scripts/`, Python 3, stdlib, no network; use `python -I`)
- `relational_lookup.py` + `ref_terms.json` + `relational_index.json` + `source_terms.json`: **three-level** lookup. **COVERED** only when the term (whole phrase, simple plural tolerated) is the **subject of a summary passage** in `references/*.md` (section title or start of the passage, in `[from source]`/`[inference]`/`[derived]`/`[DATED]` sections; index `ref_terms.json`); file name, id, title or topic keyword do **not** decide, loose words of a compound term do not either, and a passing mention in the middle of a sentence gives POINTER ONLY (the lookup shows the passage). **POINTER ONLY** (exit 3): the term appears only in a map/pointer (this INDEX, navigation maps, `pointer` topics) or only in the sources (index `source_terms.json`): the package has no summary, the agent sends the user to read the pages. **NOT COVERED** (exit 1): term in the curated out-of-the-sources list, or with no occurrence at all. Exit 0 = covered, 3 = pointer only, 1 = not covered. Since the sources and the user's term are usually in English and the summaries in Portuguese, the glossary has a section of PT/EN equivalences and `relational_index.json` keeps the `aliases` field (PT/EN equivalences **validated** against the pages: `standard practice` = `prática padrão`, `full-text search` = `busca textual`/`busca de texto`, `recovery conflict` = `conflitos de consulta`), which the lookup searches and declares in the output. Queries with `(*)`, such as `count(*)`, only match passages that carry the literal call (field `lit` of `ref_terms.json`).
- `build_term_index.py`: generates `source_terms.json` (inverted index of terms -> pages) from the extracted texts of the sources; versioned in the package.
- `build_ref_index.py`: generates `ref_terms.json` (normalized text of the passages of `references/*.md`, body x pointer class) **from the content of the references only**; must be run whenever a reference changes (the lookup warns on stderr if the index is stale; `tests/run_script_tests.py` checks that it is reproducible).
- `index_advisor.py`: index-choice heuristic; **origin of each rule and what is [extrapolation] declared in the output**.
- `tree_evaluator.py`: evaluates Adjacency List based on the excerpt (ANTI-TREE p.5-11) and declares that the other models are `[extrapolation]`.
- `injection_auditor.py`: heuristic detector of concatenation in SQL; rules with origin (ANTI-INJ) and declared limits.
- `../tests/verify_citations.py` + `citation-checks.json`: 1,013 positive and negative checks (899 positive, 114 negative; round 14 added 23 positive `R14-P01..P23` and 3 negative `R14-X01..X03`; among the older ones, of which 34 positive and 11 negative from round 10 `R10-P01..P34`/`R10-X01..X11`, 272 positive and 9 negative from round 6, including one check per command of the 3b map; negatives may have `pages` to prove that a specific page does NOT say something) against the text of the PDFs; `../tests/audit_tokens.py`: audits terms in backticks against the cited pages; `../tests/run_script_tests.py`: tests of the scripts.
